Alphabet, Google's parent company, reported its results for July, August and September (the third quarter) on 28 October, after the US market closed. Revenue grew 25% to $127.6 billion. That made it the 13th quarter in a row with growth above 10%. Google Cloud doubled its revenue.

Even so, the stock fell 12.6% the next day. It closed at $283.82, against $324.89 a day earlier. The value of all Alphabet shares fell by about $505 billion in a single day. The market did not punish Cloud, but Search, which brings in almost half of revenue. Search revenue grew 12%, after 16.8% in April to June. Among the reasons, management also admitted that the new way of searching with artificial intelligence (AI Mode) currently brings in less advertising money than classic search. At the same time, Google spent more on equipment than it generated in cash, and it is issuing new shares.

For a long-term investor, a drop of this size is a signal to check whether anything has changed in the reasons for owning the company. The analysis therefore looks for the answer to one question: **is the drop a sign that artificial intelligence is eating into Google Search, or the price of a period in which Google invests more than it earns?**

## What Alphabet said

First, what analysts expected before the results and what Alphabet delivered from July to September:

| July to September 2026 | Analysts' expectations | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $128.22bn | $127.58bn |
| Search revenue | $64.9bn | $63.35bn |
| YouTube ads revenue | $11.4bn | $11.21bn |
| Earnings per share excluding investments | $3.02 | $2.67 |
| Free cash flow | −$2.7bn | −$9.07bn |

Earnings per share is net profit after all costs and taxes, divided across all shares. Under accounting rules it was $2.12. It includes the fall in the value of Google's investment in SpaceX, which reduced it by $0.55. Analysts expected earnings excluding such effects: at $2.67, that figure was also below the expected $3.02.

What revenue is made of and how fast each part is growing:

| July to September | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search | $56.57bn | $63.35bn | +12.0% |
| YouTube ads | $10.26bn | $11.21bn | +9.2% |
| Subscriptions, platforms, devices | $12.87bn | $14.29bn | +11.0% |
| Ads on other sites (Google Network) | $7.35bn | $7.46bn | +1.4% |
| Google Cloud | $15.16bn | $30.71bn | **+103%** |
| Total revenue | $102.35bn | $127.58bn | +24.7% |

Cloud contributed 62% of all revenue growth. Without it, the revenue Alphabet groups under the name Google Services (Search, YouTube, subscriptions, devices) grew 10.6%.

Alphabet does not give a numerical revenue forecast for October to December. Chief financial officer Anat Ashkenazi said growth would be a few points lower because of a high base last year, and that currencies would weigh somewhat more, mainly on Search and YouTube. She also flagged pressure on margins, because Google is starting to rent computing power from other providers. Capital expenditure will stay at $195 to $205 billion this year, and in 2027 it will, in her words, "increase significantly".

## Why the stock fell

There are four reasons. The first is the most important, because it concerns the business most investors own Alphabet for.

**1. Search slowed, and it shows in the number of ad clicks.** Search revenue is earned when a user clicks an ad. So it can be split into two parts: how many paid clicks there were, and how much an advertiser paid per click.

![The Search slowdown is in the number of paid clicks](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/iskanje-kliki.svg)

From April to June paid clicks grew 13%, from July to September only 8%. The cost per click actually grew slightly faster than before, by 4% instead of 3%. So the slowdown is mainly in the number of clicks.

Management gave five reasons. The first was currencies, which chief business officer Philipp Schindler called the largest. The second was the EU removing a tax exemption for small parcels, which led Asian retailers to advertise less. The third was the football World Cup, which inflated advertising from April to June. The fourth was budgets that advertisers spent in June rather than July. The fifth is that the new way of searching with artificial intelligence (AI Mode) currently brings in less money.

These explanations can be partly checked. Currencies change the dollar price of a click, not the number of clicks. For total revenue, the currency effect swung from +$0.74 billion in April to June to −$0.70 billion in July to September, which explains part of the slowdown. But because it is mainly growth in the number of clicks that fell, currencies are not the whole story. Alphabet does not say how much each of the other reasons contributed.

At the same time, chief executive Sundar Pichai says the number of searches is growing at its fastest rate in five years. People are using Google more than before, while revenue is growing more slowly than in previous quarters. Earnings per search are therefore growing more slowly than before. That is the heart of the concern.

**2. Free cash flow is negative.** **Free cash flow** is the cash left after a company subtracts capital expenditure from its operating cash flow. From July to September Google spent $51.4 billion on equipment, more than twice as much as a year earlier, while it generated $42.3 billion from operations. Free cash flow was −$9.1 billion, against analysts' expectations of about −$2.7 billion. Part of the gap is a one-off: in July Google paid a $5.2 billion European Commission fine dating from 2018. Inventory, mainly TPU chips it will sell to customers, rose by $3.2 billion in three months.

**3. Earnings per share missed expectations even excluding SpaceX.** The gap between $2.67 and $3.02 came from operations, not from investments.

**4. The outlook: more investment and lower margins.** Investment will rise significantly in 2027, renting outside computing power will weigh on margins, and currencies and a high base will hold back Search growth again from October to December.

Cloud is not among the reasons for the drop. The question is whether Search is in permanent decline or in transition. The answer depends on how quickly Google makes ads in AI-powered search as profitable as they are in classic search.

## Where growth comes from

Alphabet has been through a full cycle in recent years. Revenue grew 9.8% in 2022 and 8.7% in 2023. Then growth picked up: 13.9% in 2024 and 15.1% in 2025. This year, from July to September, it rose to 25%. The acceleration did not come from advertising, but from Cloud.

![Cloud carries a growing share of revenue](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/prihodki-deli.svg)

Two years ago Cloud brought in 12.9% of revenue, now 24.1%. Google Cloud sells computing power and artificial intelligence services to businesses, and since this year also systems built on its own TPU chips (a chip Google develops for artificial intelligence as an alternative to Nvidia's). According to the company, sales of these systems were a "meaningful contributor" to growth, but it does not report them separately.

Customers have committed to far more than Cloud now earns in a year. The **backlog** (the value of signed contracts not yet recognised as revenue) stands at $560.8 billion in Cloud. A year earlier the total backlog was $157.7 billion, but since this year Alphabet also counts contracts shorter than one year, so the comparison is not exactly like for like. Just over 55% of the backlog is expected to become revenue within two years. That is about $154 billion a year on average. Cloud currently brings in about $123 billion a year (July to September, multiplied by four).

**The link with Anthropic.** One of the large buyers of computing power is Anthropic, the developer of the Claude model. In its filing to go public (S-1, 26 October 2026) it states that it has committed to spend at least about $114.5 billion with Google between April 2026 and July 2033, regardless of actual usage. For additional TPU computing power between 2028 and 2031 it estimates another $125 to $150 billion. Google invested $10 billion in Anthropic in May 2026 and can invest up to $20 billion more by the end of 2030. Its stake is capped at 14.99%. Anthropic's commitment is about one fifth of the size of the Cloud backlog. Google does not disclose how much of this commitment is already in the backlog. For Google, Anthropic is therefore a Cloud customer, a chip customer and an investment at the same time, and a competitor in models.

## Why profit is growing and what holds it back

Operating income is the profit from the business itself, before the company pays interest and taxes. From July to September it grew 29.9% to $40.6 billion. The operating margin was 31.8%: of every $100 of revenue, $31.80 remained. The breakdown shows where it came from:

| Operating income, July to September | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Services | $33.53bn | $39.16bn | +16.8% |
| Google Services excluding last year's fine | $37.03bn | $39.16bn | +5.8% |
| Google Cloud | $3.59bn | $11.12bn | 3.1 times |
| Shared AI research and other | −$4.47bn | −$7.66bn | +71% in costs |
| Waymo and other bets (Other Bets) | −$1.43bn | −$2.05bn | +44% in losses |
| Total | $31.23bn | $40.58bn | +29.9% |

Last year's Google Services profit was reduced by a one-off European Commission fine of $3.5 billion. Excluding it, the core business earned only 5.8% more than a year earlier, even though its revenue grew 10.6%. The Google Services operating margin was 40.7%, against 42.5% a year earlier excluding the fine. According to the company, profit was also held back by higher payments to partners that build Google Search into their devices and browsers; these payments grow with revenue.

![Cloud: revenue doubled, operating income tripled](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/oblak.svg)

Profit growth is now carried by Cloud. Its margin rose from 23.7% to 36.2%. On the other side are costs that Alphabet does not allocate to its businesses: mainly shared artificial intelligence research, the development of the Gemini models. They rose 71% to $7.7 billion in three months.

The pressure point is **depreciation**: the cost of equipment does not appear in the income statement when it is bought, but is spread over the years it is used. From July to September it rose 49% to $8.4 billion. Because Google is investing about $200 billion this year, this cost will keep rising for years, regardless of how fast revenue grows.

## How much cash remains

![Investment has outgrown operating cash flow](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/denar-nalozbe.svg)

In 2025 Google was left with $73.3 billion of free cash flow. Over the last 12 months, only $19.7 billion. From July to September capital expenditure reached 40% of revenue, against 15% two years ago. According to the chief financial officer, about 65% of the investment goes to servers and network equipment, the rest to data centres.

Because operating cash flow is not enough, Alphabet has changed how it finances itself. In 2025 it spent $45.7 billion buying back its own shares, this year nothing. Instead, in the first nine months it:

- raised $42.9 billion with new shares, partly through ongoing sales on the stock market (an ATM programme);
- issued preferred shares that will convert into common shares, for $19.1 billion;
- borrowed $85.2 billion (and repaid $8.9 billion).

The number of shares has risen 1.7% since the end of 2025. The dividend remains $0.22 per share per quarter. For equipment, energy and content, Google has signed purchase commitments worth $924.4 billion. Of that, it will have to pay $213.4 billion over the next year.

One-off items distort the figures:

| One-off item | Amount | What it affects |
|---|---|---|
| European Commission fine (July to September 2025) | charge of $3.5bn | last year's Google Services profit |
| Payment of the 2018 fine (July 2026) | $5.2bn | free cash flow, July to September 2026 |
| Fall in the value of the SpaceX investment (July to September 2026) | $8.7bn, $0.55 per share | net profit |
| Gains on investments (January to June 2026) | other income of $135.7bn | net profit |

Because of the investments in SpaceX and Anthropic, net profit is a poor measure of the business this year. Operating income is a better guide.

How much does capital earn? **Return on invested capital (ROIC)** shows how much after-tax profit every dollar invested in the business by owners and lenders generates. Over the last 12 months it was about 32% (Engineering Investor calculation). Google is now investing more than it earns after tax: investment amounts to 108% of after-tax operating income. At 22% growth, that means new investment is earning about 20%, less than the average on existing capital. That is still a good return, but it is falling.

## Management and direction

Management has shown its direction clearly through its actions. First, it is investing as much as it can, because, according to the chief financial officer, demand for computing power exceeds supply. Second, it now also sells TPU chips to outside customers and to other companies' data centres, including through a partnership with Oracle and Crux AI, a joint venture with Blackstone. Third, it has replaced share buybacks with issues of shares and debt. Fourth, it is extending ads in AI-powered search to more users and, for the first time, outside the US.

Through these actions management is saying it believes the investment will earn more than the new capital costs. Whether it is right will be shown by Cloud and by what Search earns. This is where opinions divide.

## The bear case: artificial intelligence takes money from Search, and investment earns less

The bear case is the view of those who expect harder times. At Alphabet it has two parts.

The first is Search. People ask more and more of their questions to chatbots and new forms of search, where they get an answer without clicking an ad. Management itself admits that AI Mode currently brings in less money. Growth in paid clicks fell from 13% to 8%, and that is not caused by currencies. Search brings in almost half of revenue and has so far been the most profitable part of the business. If earnings per search fall permanently, Cloud cannot replace all of it.

The second part is investment. The case runs like this:

1. Investment is growing faster than revenue: $160 billion over the last 12 months, and more in 2027.
2. New investment earns less than existing capital, about 20% instead of 32%.
3. Depreciation will hold down the margin for years, even if revenue growth falls.
4. Commitments of $924 billion remain even if demand for artificial intelligence cools.

The result would be a company with a lower margin, less cash for shareholders and more shares.

## The bull case: more searches, ads still to come, Cloud growing

The bulls do not deny that Search has slowed. They argue that it is a transition, not a decline.

**If people were leaving Google, the number of searches would fall.** According to Pichai, however, searches are growing at their fastest rate in five years, including those with buying intent. Management says most searches in AI Mode are additional: people are not using them instead of classic search. So people are not leaving. The problem is how much Google earns per search, and that is solvable.

**Ads in the new search are only now arriving.** Google is extending them in AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and to foreign markets. According to Schindler, people in AI Mode click on advertised products about as often as on non-advertised ones. Ads in classic search are refined; in the new search they are only being introduced.

**Cloud is not a bet, but a business.** Revenue doubled, the margin is 36%, and the backlog is $561 billion. The Gemini app has a billion users, and Google's models process almost 32 billion tokens (units of text) per minute, more than four times as many as a year ago.

**Google has every layer of artificial intelligence.** Its own chips, models, cloud and access to billions of users through Search, Android, YouTube and Chrome. That gives it an advantage over companies that have to rent computing power.

## What the price already includes

![Alphabet: share price over the last 12 months](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/cena-12-mesecev.svg)

After the drop, the stock is 29.5% below its peak of 13 May 2026 and 9.3% lower than at the end of 2025. At $283.82 all shares are worth about $3,489 billion. Of that, about $257 billion is cash and investments, including SpaceX shares and the stake in Anthropic, minus debt.

What the price expects is shown by a **reverse DCF**. Instead of forecasting the future and calculating a value, we take the price and calculate what revenue growth it already includes. The model takes into account that growth costs money: part of the profit has to go into investment. The required return is the return an investor wants each year.

| Required return | Price before results ($324.89) | Price after the drop ($283.82) |
|---|---|---|
| 8% | 13.3% | 10.4% |
| 9% | 17.3% | **14.3%** |
| 10% | 21.2% | 18.0% |

The table shows the revenue growth per year over the next five years that the price includes, if the operating margin stays at 33.3%. After five years, growth gradually falls to 2.5%.

![After the drop, the price expects less growth](/images/articles/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/obratni-dcf.svg)

At a 9% required return, the price before the results included about 17% revenue growth a year over five years. After the drop, only about 14%. That is less than analysts expect for this year and next, but more than the 2021 to 2025 average (11.8% a year). So the market lowered its expectations, but did not bring them down to a level that would require only past growth. If new investment earns only about 20%, the price requires even more growth. Before the results that would be about 20.5% a year, after the drop 17.2%.

What an investor gets at the price after the drop if one of three scenarios plays out (an illustration, not a forecast):

| Scenario for the next five years | Revenue growth per year | Operating margin | Annual return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cautious | 10% | 30% | 7.4% |
| Central | 15% | 33% | 9.1% |
| Favourable | 20% | 36% | 11.0% |

This creates an asymmetry, meaning a difference between how much you can lose and how much you can gain. On the downside: if Search growth keeps slowing and depreciation pushes the margin down, the return is about 7% a year (the cautious scenario), and less in a worse outcome. On the upside: Cloud and chips grow faster than the central scenario assumes, and ads in the new search catch up with classic ones. Which outcome is closer will be decided by what Search earns and what the investment returns.

## What to watch to know who is right

All five points are different views of the same question: is artificial intelligence taking money from Search or only reshaping it, and does the investment pay off?

1. **Paid clicks and cost per click** (quarterly Form 10-Q). If click growth returns above 10%, management was right that the causes were temporary.
2. **What ads in AI Mode earn.** Management currently describes this only in words. The first figure on revenue or earnings per search will matter.
3. **Cloud backlog and margin.** Renting outside computing power will weigh on the margin. It needs to stay well above 30%.
4. **The 2027 capital expenditure outlook and free cash flow.** The outlook comes in February 2027 with the results for October to December.
5. **The number of shares.** Whether share sales on the stock market and the conversion of preferred shares dilute existing shareholders faster than profit grows.

The next milestone is the results for October to December, which Alphabet will publish in early February 2027.

## A conclusion everyone has to draw for themselves

July, August and September showed two sides of Alphabet. Cloud has gone from a side business to the company's engine: revenue doubled, profit tripled, and customers have committed $561 billion. Search, however, clearly stalled for the first time: paid clicks are growing more slowly, management admits AI Mode earns less, and the core business excluding last year's fine earns only 5.8% more than a year ago. At the same time, Google is investing so much that it has no cash left over, and it is issuing new shares.

Anyone who thinks the Search slowdown is temporary today gets a company whose price includes less growth than analysts expect, and which has every layer of artificial intelligence under one roof. Anyone who thinks it is the beginning of a decline sees investment that earns less and less, $924 billion of commitments, and the most profitable part of the company in a transition with no guaranteed end. Which side is right will be shown by growth in paid clicks and by free cash flow in 2027.
