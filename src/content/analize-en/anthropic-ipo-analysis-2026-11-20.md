Anthropic, the company that develops the Claude artificial intelligence models, began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on 19 November. It sold shares at $83 and, together with a concurrent private placement, raised $107.3bn. According to The National, this is the largest IPO so far; the previous record was set by SpaceX in June with $86bn.

On its first day the stock opened at $111.50, 34% above the offer price, and closed at $97.17, 17% above it [graf](#graf-prvi-dan). At the close the company was worth about $1.3 trillion, counting the shares in issue. This analysis was written on the morning of 20 November, Central European time.

## How the first day went

Trading only began at around 17:50 Central European time. Within half an hour the stock fell from $111.50 to about $96. According to NBC, the S&P 500, the Nasdaq and the Dow Jones all lost more than 2% that day, partly because investors feared that Nvidia's China export problems extend to other AI companies. The media give no specific reason for the fall in Anthropic's shares.

Demand was strong: according to a PitchBook analyst, orders were about six times the number of shares on offer. Before the market opened on 20 November, the stock traded at around $91, 6% below its first close.

## How Anthropic makes money

Anthropic develops AI models and sells them to companies and individuals. It earns most of its revenue in two ways:

- **By usage.** Companies pay through an application programming interface for each task the model performs. This revenue grows with usage.
- **Through subscriptions.** Individuals and companies pay a subscription for applications such as Claude and Claude Code.

Claude is also available in the clouds of Amazon, Google and Microsoft. There, Anthropic contracts directly with the customer and pays the cloud provider a fee. Fees paid to the partners through which it sells rose by $4.8bn in the first nine months of 2026 and are the main reason for the increase in sales costs.

No single customer accounted for more than 10% of revenue. At the end of September there were more than 11,900 customers that had paid over $100,000 in the past year, against about 950 a year earlier.

## How fast it is growing

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 (Q3 2026) reached $17.0bn, against $1.4bn a year earlier [graf](#graf-prihodki). That is more than 12 times as much. According to the company, **run-rate revenue** reached $81bn at the end of October; it is usage over the last 28 days plus current subscriptions, annualised, and not a forecast.

| Revenue ($bn) | 2024 | 2025 | 9 months 2025 | 9 months 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 0.4 | 4.6 | 2.5 | 33.2 |
| Compute and infrastructure costs | 2.5 | 7.3 | 4.4 | 18.8 |
| Operating income | −3.0 | −8.1 | −5.2 | −1.3 |

Growth on the previous quarter eased in Q3: revenue rose 47%, against 144% in Q2. The company writes that customers are less active during the summer and winter holidays; it does not say whether this explains the slowdown in Q3. It also writes that models released in the last six months accounted for more than 80% of Q3 revenue. Growth therefore depends heavily on each new model unlocking new uses.

**Net revenue retention** shows how much more existing customers paid than a year earlier. At Anthropic it is about 900%: customers that were already there a year earlier paid roughly nine times as much.

## Is it profitable yet

Almost. **Operating income**, revenue minus operating costs, turned positive for the first time in Q2 2026 at $1.0bn, then negative again in Q3 at −$0.6bn [graf](#graf-poslovni-izid). In Q3, compute and infrastructure costs rose from $5.6bn to $9.9bn. Other costs rose from $1.0bn to $3.6bn; according to the company, the largest of them are non-cash costs of shares it uses to match employees' donations.

The largest cost is **compute**: the chips and data centres on which models are trained and run. In the first nine months of 2025 it cost 172% of revenue, in the first nine months of 2026 57% [graf](#graf-racunska-moc). The company attributes this mainly to revenue growing faster than the capacity it had committed in advance. It had to buy part of its capacity, $6.5bn, on short-term terms, which it says is considerably more expensive.

The net loss for the first nine months of 2026 was $104bn, but most of it is not a cash outflow. **Convertible notes**, through which Anthropic raised money from Amazon, can be converted into shares. As the company's value rose, so did the value of the notes, which shows up in the income statement as a loss: $94.3bn together with similar items. Excluding such items, adjusted operating income was positive at $4.1bn, or 12% of revenue. Cash flow from operations was $6.6bn.

## Compute commitments

| Commitments and cash | Amount |
|---|---|
| All commitments, mainly compute and infrastructure | $900.9bn |
| of which by the end of 2030 | $451.6bn |
| of which in the 12 months after 30 Sep 2026 | $70.7bn |
| Cash and short-term investments, 30 Sep 2026 | $131.9bn |
| Net proceeds of the offering and private placement | $104.8bn |

Anthropic has committed to spending $900.9bn on compute through 2045. That is about 11 times its run-rate revenue. It buys capacity from several providers and uses chips from Amazon, Google, Nvidia and AMD. After the offering it has about $237bn of cash. That is more than three times the commitments for the next year and roughly half of the commitments through the end of 2030.

This is the core of the investment story. If revenue keeps growing as it has over the past year, there is not enough capacity. If growth stops, Anthropic will be paying for capacity it cannot sell.

## Who controls the company

A buyer of a Class A share gets a stake in the profits, but almost no control over the company:

| Share class | Who holds it | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| A | public, employees, investors | one vote per share |
| F (one share) | the seven co-founders' company | 50.1% of all votes |
| T (one share) | Long-Term Benefit Trust | elects a majority of the board |
| N | Amazon, Google and other investors | no voting rights |

Anthropic is a **public benefit corporation**: by law, its board must balance the interests of shareholders, of those affected by the company, and the purpose set out in its charter. That purpose is the responsible development of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. The **Long-Term Benefit Trust** is a trust of five trustees with no financial interest in the company, and it elects a majority of the board. Through the Class F share, the co-founders hold a majority of votes on all other matters.

Shareholders should also be aware of dilution. The company has set aside 5% of its shares for its public benefit fund. At its discretion, the board may grant the fund up to another 2% of shares each time the price rises 50% above the previous threshold, starting from the offer price.

## Who the owners are

By our calculations, Amazon holds about 20% of the shares after dilution and Google about 10%. In the private placement alongside the offering, Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC bought shares worth $26bn; Google and Nvidia $10bn each. Several of them are also Anthropic's suppliers of chips or compute.

About 6% of the shares after dilution were offered to the public. Most of the rest are locked up for 180 days. According to NBC, employees may sell 10% of their shares on the first day and up to another 35% after the annual results are published, before the end of March 2027. A PitchBook analyst told The National that the first year will test the price as the restrictions are lifted in stages.

## What it is worth

| Value at the close on 19 Nov | Amount | Versus run-rate revenue ($81bn) |
|---|---|---|
| Shares in issue | about $1,285bn | 16 times |
| Fully diluted | about $1,707bn | 21 times |
| For comparison: Nvidia, 17 Nov | about $3,760bn | 10 times annual revenue |

**Fully diluted** means we also count shares not yet issued: options and RSUs for employees, and shares from the Amazon notes not yet converted. The media cite about $1.75 trillion.

Investors are therefore paying about $21 for each dollar of Anthropic's run-rate revenue, and about $10 for each dollar of Nvidia's revenue over the last year. Nvidia also has a 67% operating margin, while Anthropic's is around zero. The price therefore assumes that Anthropic will keep growing very fast for several more years and that its margin will later be high.

## The bear case: growth can stop, the commitments remain

**Growth depends on the next model.** More than 80% of Q3 revenue came from models less than six months old. If competitors such as OpenAI and Google offered better or cheaper models, customers who pay by usage could move quickly.

**The commitments are enormous.** $900.9bn is a long-term obligation regardless of how fast revenue grows. At the same time, Anthropic is cutting prices per task, which increases usage but can reduce revenue per customer.

**Shareholders have no influence.** Decisions are made by the co-founders and the trust, which must also weigh interests other than profit. Legal disputes are another risk: in September 2025 the company settled the Bartz copyright class action for $1.5bn.

## The bull case: exceptional growth and close to profit

**Revenue is growing exceptionally fast.** In 18 months it rose from $0.4bn to $17.0bn a quarter. There are more than 11,900 larger customers, none accounting for more than 10% of revenue.

**The business is already close to profit.** Compute costs are falling relative to revenue, adjusted operating income is positive, and so is cash flow from operations. After the offering, the company has enough cash for several years of buildout.

**Strategic partners are also owners.** Amazon, Google, Nvidia and Microsoft have invested money and have an interest in Anthropic succeeding.

## A conclusion everyone has to draw for themselves

Anthropic came to the market as a company growing exceptionally fast and close to its first profit. At the same time, relative to revenue it is twice as expensive as Nvidia, with commitments 11 times its annual revenue and a governance structure in which public shareholders have almost no say.

Those who believe Claude will remain among the leading models and that companies will use AI more and more see a chance to buy a stake in one of the winners. The cautious see a price that already assumes several years of exceptional growth, and a first year in which the selling restrictions will be lifted. The first answers will come with the Q4 2026 results, the first after the IPO.

## Key takeaways

- Offering: 980m shares at $83 ($81.3bn) and a $26bn private placement; $107.3bn in total.
- First day: open $111.50 (+34%), close $97.17 (+17%); the indices lost more than 2% that day.
- Value at the close: about $1,285bn (shares in issue), about $1,707bn fully diluted.
- Q3 2026 revenue: $17.0bn (Q3 2025: $1.4bn); run-rate revenue $81bn at the end of October.
- Growth on the previous quarter eased to 47% in Q3 (Q2: 144%).
- Q3 operating income: −$0.6bn; adjusted operating income for nine months +$4.1bn.
- Compute commitments: $900.9bn, of which $70.7bn in the next year; cash after the offering about $237bn.
- The co-founders hold 50.1% of the votes, and the trust elects a majority of the board.
- After dilution, Amazon holds about 20% and Google about 10%.
