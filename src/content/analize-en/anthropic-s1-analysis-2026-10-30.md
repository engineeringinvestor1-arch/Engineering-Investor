Anthropic, the company that develops the Claude artificial intelligence models, filed for an initial public offering with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on 26 October. The filing is called an S-1. In it, a company discloses audited financial statements, risks and its governance before it sells shares to the public. The shares are to be listed on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The filing does not yet include the share price or the number of shares to be sold.

The filing runs to more than 400 pages. This overview gathers the most important parts in numbers and charts: where Anthropic stands today and what the filing says about where artificial intelligence is heading. Data for 2023 to 2025 is audited; this year's is not, which is normal for an S-1.

## In brief

| What | Number |
|---|---|
| Revenue, April to June 2026 | $11.6bn, 14.7 times more than a year earlier |
| Operating result, April to June 2026 | positive for the first time, 8% margin |
| Operating cash flow, January to June 2026 | $3.9bn |
| Net loss, January to June 2026 | $80.7bn, almost all on paper |
| Customers paying at least $100,000 a year | more than 11,900 (a year earlier more than 950) |
| Cash and short-term investments, 30 Jun 2026 | $131.7bn |
| Non-cancelable compute commitments, 30 Jun 2026 | about $265.6bn |
| Employees | about 5,300 |
| Co-founders' share of shareholder votes | 50.1% |

## Revenue is growing faster than the cost of compute

![Revenue has overtaken the cost of compute](/images/articles/anthropic-s1-analysis-2026-10-30/prihodki-racunska-moc.svg)

Revenue rose from $41 million to $386 million in 2024 and to $4.6 billion in 2025. This year growth accelerated further. Compared with the previous quarter, revenue grew:

- October to December 2025: +48%,
- January to March 2026: +132%,
- April to June 2026: +144%.

The company attributes the acceleration to new models. The growth resembles a multi-stage rocket: each new model is a new stage that ignites before the previous one burns out. Models released in the six months to the end of September 2026 brought in more than 80% of revenue from July to September.

**Compute** means the chips, servers and power a model needs to be trained and to answer customers. Anthropic secures it through multi-year contracts, 12 to 18 months before it receives it. The cost therefore rises when the capacity arrives, not when the customers do. In January to March 2025, compute cost was 251% of revenue; in April to June 2026 only 48%. When costs are fixed in the short term, profit grows faster than revenue; this is called **operating leverage**.

| Quarter | Operating margin | Adjusted margin |
|---|---|---|
| January to March 2025 | −249% | −221% |
| April to June 2025 | −152% | −129% |
| July to September 2025 | −224% | −93% |
| October to December 2025 | −139% | −84% |
| January to March 2026 | −35% | −13% |
| April to June 2026 | 8% | 19% |

The **margin** shows how much of every $100 of revenue is left as profit. The adjusted margin is the company's own measure: it excludes employee pay in shares, donations in shares and a one-off copyright settlement (the Bartz case, $1.5 billion). Pay in shares is a real cost, because new shares reduce the stake of existing owners (**dilution**). The company warns that the margin will fluctuate: when a large new block of compute arrives, the cost comes before the revenue.

## Who pays, and how

![The number of large customers grew more than 12-fold in a year](/images/articles/anthropic-s1-analysis-2026-10-30/kupci.svg)

The money comes mainly from businesses. Customers paying at least $100,000 a year brought in 74% of revenue in the first half of the year. They include all ten of the largest US companies on the Fortune list. Existing customers keep spending more: **net revenue retention** (NRR), which shows how much the same group of customers pays today compared with a year ago, is about 900% according to the company.

- **Pay for usage.** A model cuts text into **tokens**, small units it reads and writes, and the customer pays for the tokens used. In the first half of 2026, 80% of revenue came from usage and 20% from subscriptions.
- **Through the clouds.** 48% of sales go through the cloud marketplaces of Amazon, Google and Microsoft.
- **Large customers.** More than 1,900 customers pay at least $1 million a year, and about 250 at least $10 million. The five largest bring in about 25% of revenue, none more than 10%.
- **Policy violators.** The company estimates that 5% to 7% of revenue in the first nine months of the year came from customers who violate its usage policy. This revenue will decline.
- **Holidays.** In the two weeks to 2 January 2026, usage fell by about 20% compared with the two weeks before.

## Why an $80 billion loss if the business makes money

![Almost all of the loss is on paper, not in cash](/images/articles/anthropic-s1-analysis-2026-10-30/izguba-most.svg)

Most of the loss comes from **convertible notes**: a loan that converts into shares at the IPO. Anthropic issued $6.2 billion of them to Amazon. They are carried in the books at the value of the shares they will convert into, so their value rose to $108.6 billion by 30 June 2026. The company booked the increase as an expense, but no cash left the company. It is like promising a friend part of your house: when the house rises in value, your obligation in the books grows, even though you have paid nothing.

At the IPO the notes convert into shares and the effect largely disappears. Real cash is positive: operating cash flow from January to June was $3.9 billion. A warning for the future: employee pay in shares that the company has not yet booked amounts to $26.6 billion. A large part will be recognised in the quarter of the IPO.

## Cash and compute

Since it was founded, Anthropic has raised $141.1 billion of capital, most of it this year:

| Funding round (2026) | Raised | Price per share |
|---|---|---|
| Series G (January, February) | $30.2bn | $25.91 |
| Series G-2, Google and Amazon (April to June) | $15.0bn | $25.91 |
| Series H (May, June) | $65.2bn | $58.90 |

The prices are adjusted for the 10-for-1 stock split of September 2026. At the end of June the company had $131.7 billion of cash and short-term investments. Its commitments are even larger.

![Compute commitments with eight partners](/images/articles/anthropic-s1-analysis-2026-10-30/zaveze.svg)

Non-cancelable commitments grew from $54.6 billion at the end of 2025 to about $265.6 billion on 30 June 2026. That is what the company would earn in almost six years at its April to June pace. After 30 June it added minimum payments of about $245 billion long-term and $21 billion short-term. With Google and Amazon it must pay the difference even if it uses less compute.

More and more compute will be leased directly, in data centres operated by others on its behalf (Anthropic Direct). It secures the space early and picks the expensive chips later. It is similar to an airline that orders aircraft years in advance: if the passengers come, it earns a lot; if they don't, it still has to pay for the aircraft. Google is at once a supplier, an investor and a competitor; what this means for Google is covered in our [analysis of Alphabet](/en/analysis/alphabet-analysis-2026-10-29/).

## Who is in control

A buyer of shares on the stock market will get a share of the profits, but not control of the company.

- **Public benefit corporation** (PBC). The board must balance the interests of shareholders, of those affected by the company, and the public benefit: the responsible development of advanced AI.
- **Co-founders.** The seven co-founders will hold 50.1% of the votes at shareholder meetings through a special entity.
- **Independent trust.** The Long-Term Benefit Trust, with five trustees who have no financial stake, elects a majority of the board.
- **Public benefit fund.** It received 846.8 million shares, 5% of the total, worth about $50 billion at the Series H price.
- **Lock-up.** Larger owners may sell after the results for July to September 2027 are published, the others after the results for January to March 2027 (or earlier, after 365 or 180 days).

## Main risks

| Risk | What the filing says |
|---|---|
| Compute commitments | Mostly non-cancelable; they must be paid regardless of revenue. |
| Competitors | OpenAI, Google and open models; more than 80% of revenue comes from models less than six months old. |
| US government | The Department of War designated it a supply-chain risk in February; an appeals court upheld the designation 2 to 1 in September, and proceedings continue. |
| Export restrictions | In June 2026, restrictions on the Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models, which were switched off for all customers until they were lifted. |
| Dependence on clouds | Almost half of sales go through cloud providers that have their own models. |
| Copyright | Bartz settlement of $1.5bn; music publishers and Reddit are also suing. |
| Dilution | $26.6bn of unbooked share-based pay, extra shares for the fund. |

## What it could be worth

There is no IPO price yet. The Series H price of $58.90 multiplied by about 16.9 billion total shares gives about $1,000 billion. Media report that the company is aiming for $1,500 to $2,000 billion; this is not company data. For annual revenue we take revenue for April to June multiplied by four ($46.2 billion).

| Company value | Price per share | Multiple of annual revenue |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000bn | $59.2 | 21.6 times |
| $1,500bn | $88.8 | 32.5 times |
| $2,000bn | $118.3 | 43.3 times |

A multiple does not say how fast revenue has to grow. A **reverse DCF** shows that: it takes the company's value and calculates what future it already includes.

![What revenue the valuation implies](/images/articles/anthropic-s1-analysis-2026-10-30/obratni-dcf.svg)

At $1,000 billion, Anthropic would need about $258 billion of annual revenue in five years, which means growth of about 41% a year. At $2,000 billion, about $464 billion, almost as much as Alphabet, Google's parent company, has today.

## What the filing says about the future of AI

The filing is about one company, but it is the first audited look at the finances of a developer of the most advanced models. It shows seven directions that apply to the whole industry.

1. **From chat to digital colleagues.** The money no longer comes only from answering questions, but from agents that do the work themselves. Claude Code plans, writes, tests and fixes software code. Claude Cowork does the same with office work: it reads files and works in applications. Claude Enterprise users without Claude Code spent less than $75 a month from July to September; the most active 5% with Claude Code spent about 20 times that.
2. **Tasks keep getting cheaper, usage keeps growing.** In September 2026 Opus 5.5 cut the cost per task by about 40% compared with Opus 5, and Fable 5.1 by about 25% compared with Fable 5. When Opus 4.5 brought a similar price cut, revenue growth accelerated from 48% to 132% a quarter.
3. **Businesses are only at the start.** Customers paying at least $100,000 make up more than half of the Fortune 500. At these companies, Anthropic receives on average less than 0.1% of their total expenses.
4. **The bottleneck is physical.** According to the company, demand for compute has long exceeded the capacity available to it. The constraints are chips and memory made by few manufacturers in few places, power and data centres. Hence commitments of hundreds of billions.
5. **AI helps develop AI.** More than 90% of contributions to the company's code in September 2026 were written by Claude. The company says its models are speeding up its research, and lists as a risk that progress could outpace its ability to check safety.
6. **The most capable models will not be available to everyone.** In April 2026, the Mythos model was first given only to a few selected organisations for cybersecurity, because it can find vulnerabilities in software. The company expects similar access limits for future models, and the state adds its own through export restrictions.
7. **The lead is short, and the giants are intertwined.** Customers move quickly to a newer model. Some cloud providers are at once Anthropic's compute suppliers, a sales channel, customers, investors and competitors.

All seven directions lead to the same question: will demand for AI grow at least as fast as the compute commitments? If it does, the commitments are a source of advantage. If it doesn't, they become a burden that still has to be paid.

## What to watch

1. **Price range and number of shares** in the amendment to the filing (S-1/A).
2. **Revenue for July to September 2026**, which the filing does not yet include.
3. **The margin when new compute arrives**, for example about $3.1 billion a month at SpaceXAI from December 2026.
4. **The share of policy violators** and the number of customers above $100,000.
5. **The dispute with the US government** and new export restrictions.
6. **The cost of share-based pay** in the quarter of the IPO and the end of the lock-up in 2027.
