The S&P 500, which combines 500 large US companies, fell for a sixth straight day on Thursday, 19 November. It lost 5.1% over the week and is 8.2% below its 6 October peak [graf](#graf-sp500-vix). The **VIX index**, which measures how large the swings investors expect over the next month are, and is therefore called the fear index, closed at 28.44. That is the highest in the last six months. Asian markets also fell on Friday: Japan's Nikkei by 5.1% and South Korea's Kospi by 5.2%.

This article was written on the morning of 20 November, Central European time, before the New York market opened. We will review the whole week in Saturday's weekly analysis.

## How markets moved

| Market | Week to 19 Nov | From the peak of the last six months |
|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | −5.1% | −8.2% (peak 6 Oct) |
| Nasdaq 100 (technology companies) | −5.1% | −15.1% (peak 6 Oct) |
| Dow Jones | −6.2% | −9.7% (peak 5 Aug) |
| Euro Stoxx 50 (Europe) | −4.3% | −6.7% (peak 11 Aug) |
| Nvidia | −10.4% | −39.0% (peak 6 Oct) |
| Gold | −5.2% | −21.5% (peak 24 Aug) |
| Brent crude | −4.0% | −21.8% (peak 30 Oct) |
| Bitcoin | −9.3% | −28.5% (peak 21 Sep) |

The Nasdaq 100 is more than 10% below its peak, which the market calls a **correction**. On Thursday the Dow Jones closed below 50,000 points for the first time since June. It is unusual for stocks, gold and bitcoin to fall at the same time, as in calmer periods they often move differently.

## First cause: interest rates may go higher

US inflation data published on 10 November came in above expectations, so investors, according to Reuters, expect the US central bank, the Fed, to raise its interest rate by 0.25 percentage points in December. According to an analysis by Saxo Bank, markets expect at least two further increases by mid-2027. The Fed meets on 9 December.

Higher interest rates affect stocks in two ways. Profits that companies expect several years from now are worth less today. At the same time, a safe 10-year US government bond yields about 5% a year, which makes riskier investments less attractive. According to Reuters, the dollar is at its strongest since April 2025.

Thursday's data added to the concerns. The manufacturing index for the Philadelphia region fell from 20.9 to 6.3, which means weaker business activity. The prices companies pay, however, rose, according to Saxo. Weaker activity alongside higher prices is a difficult combination for a central bank.

## Second cause: doubts about artificial intelligence

Nvidia lost 10.4% over the week, even though it reported results above expectations on 17 November ([our analysis](/en/analysis/nvidia-analysis-2026-11-18/)). It is 39% below its October peak, and since Jensen Huang's resignation the company has been run by an interim chief executive. AMD shares fell 7.7% on Thursday, according to Saxo, after the research firm Hunterbrook disclosed a bet against the stock and allegations of breaches of US export restrictions on China.

On the same day Anthropic completed the largest IPO so far ([our analysis](/en/analysis/anthropic-ipo-analysis-2026-11-20/)). The stock opened 34% above the offer price but gave back part of the gain, while the indices fell more than 2% that day. Steve Sosnick of Interactive Brokers told CNBC that traders are using every rally to sell.

According to CNBC, on Wednesday the S&P 500 closed below its **200-day average** for the first time since 7 April. That is the average of the last 200 closes, which many investors watch as a sign of the long-term trend.

## Third cause: selling anything that can be sold quickly

Gold, which investors often buy for safety, fell more than 5% this week. Saxo writes of a dash for cash: investors are selling assets that can be sold quickly to raise money. Bitcoin fell to its lowest level since July, and according to Saxo, $582m flowed out of the ETFs that invest in it on Wednesday alone. Oil got cheaper because, according to Saxo, more oil is coming from the Middle East.

When stocks, gold and bitcoin fall at the same time, it can mean investors need cash, for example to cover losses or loans elsewhere. Such selling is fast and does not distinguish between good and bad investments.

## What this means for a long-term investor

For a long-term investor, the key question is whether companies' profits are changing or only the price investors are willing to pay for them. The latest results, including Nvidia's, show that profits are still growing. What has changed is mainly the price of money, that is, interest rates, and confidence that the large investments in artificial intelligence will pay off.

## Key takeaways

- The S&P 500 fell for a sixth straight day, by 5.1% in the week to 19 Nov; it is 8.2% below its peak.
- The Nasdaq 100 is 15.1% below its peak (a correction), Nvidia 39%.
- The VIX fear index closed at 28.44, the highest in the last six months.
- Gold (−5.2%), oil (−4.0%) and bitcoin (−9.3%) fell at the same time.
- Causes: an expected rate rise in December, doubts about artificial intelligence and selling for cash.
- On Friday the Nikkei and the Kospi each lost more than 5%.
