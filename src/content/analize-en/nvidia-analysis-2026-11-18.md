Nvidia reported results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (Q3 FY27, August to October 2026) after the market close on 17 November. Revenue was $115.8bn. That is 7% more than the company forecast in August and 6% more than analysts expected. Revenue growth was 103%, against 106% in Q2 [graf](#graf-kazalnik-prihodki). The earnings release was the first to feature Colette Kress, who has been running the company on an interim basis since Jensen Huang resigned.

The stock closed at $155.46 on 17 November, before the release, 35% below its 6 October peak [graf](#graf-cena-12-mesecev). Since our previous analysis on 12 November it had lost another 11%. After the release it rose by up to 5%, but on Wednesday it gave up the gain and traded about 2% below its pre-release close.

## What happened to the leadership

On 11 November the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, chief executive and board member, and announced it the following day. The stock fell 4.3% on 12 November. Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, became interim chief executive and remains CFO. The board is searching for a permanent successor and says it will consider internal and external candidates. According to CNBC, it has hired the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles.

For the first time the board has a separate chair, Harvey C. Jones, a former chief executive of Synopsys and a member of Nvidia's board since 1993. The board expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption. On 17 November, according to CNBC, Huang said he would return if the board decided so. On the call with analysts, Jones replied that the board does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive.

## Q3 FY27 results

| Q3 ($bn) | Q3 FY26 | Q3 FY27 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue [graf](#graf-kazalnik-prihodki) | 57.0 | 115.8 | +103% |
| Data centre | 51.2 | 107.6 | +110% |
| of which large cloud providers | 35.1 | 60.1 | +71% |
| of which other clouds, enterprises, governments | 16.1 | 47.5 | +195% |
| Edge computing (PCs, cars, robots) | 5.8 | 8.2 | +42% |
| Gross margin | 73.4% | 74.8% | +1.4 points |
| Operating income [graf](#graf-kazalnik-dobicek) | 36.0 | 77.5 | +115% |
| Free cash flow [graf](#graf-kazalnik-fcf) | 22.1 | 43.8 | +98% |

**Gross margin** shows how much of each $100 of revenue is left after the cost of making the products: $75. **Operating income** is the profit after all operating costs; $67 of each $100 of revenue was left, the most in the last five years. **Free cash flow** is the cash from operations after spending on equipment.

Growth eased slightly for the first time after four consecutive accelerations, but revenue rose 20% on the previous quarter, against 18% in Q2. According to Nvidia, the biggest contributor was the launch of its new Vera Rubin chip generation, which it calls the fastest ramp in the company's history.

Part of the beat came from China. Nvidia shipped older Hopper chips worth $2.4bn there, while its forecast had assumed no data centre revenue from China. Without them, revenue would have been 5% above the forecast.

## What analysts expected

| Q3 FY27 | Analyst expectations | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $109.1bn | $115.8bn |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $2.48 | $2.68 |
| Other clouds, enterprises, governments | about $49.0bn | $47.5bn |
| Q4 revenue guidance | $125.1bn | $130bn |
| Q4 gross margin guidance | 71.7% | 71% to 72% |

Nvidia beat expectations everywhere except with the other customers, the fastest-growing group. The gross margin guidance was in line with expectations. **Adjusted earnings** exclude gains and losses on investments in other companies and some one-off items.

After the release the stock rose by up to 5% by midnight Central European time. On Wednesday, after the market opened, it gave up that gain. The media do not give a clear reason. Shares of specialised cloud providers, which are Nvidia customers, also fell: CoreWeave by more than 6%, according to CNBC. Jefferies wrote that questions remain about the pace of the data centre buildout and a possible sudden slowdown.

## Who is buying

Large cloud providers bought $60.1bn; Nvidia does not name them. Their growth on a year earlier slowed from 102% to 71%, mainly because of a high base: in Q3 FY26 they bought 45% more than in the quarter before. Compared with Q2, they bought 23% more this year.

Other customers are growing faster: specialised AI clouds (neoclouds), model developers, enterprises and governments. Their purchases nearly tripled in a year, and their share of data centre revenue rose from 31% to 44%. These are exactly the customers Nvidia increasingly helps to finance their buildout. Nvidia itself writes that many are growing faster than their balance sheets allow. On the call, Kress said Nvidia would be "disciplined and selective" in that support. Developers of the most capable models will, she said, bring in about 15% of data centre revenue this year.

Dependence on a few customers remains high. The largest direct customer accounted for 14% of revenue in Q3, the next two for 11% each. In the first nine months, four customers together accounted for 48%. Two customers owe 35% of all receivables. Direct customers include server makers that build the chips into systems for others, so they are not necessarily the end users.

## What the outlook for Q4 and next year says

For Q4 FY27 (November 2026 to January 2027) Nvidia forecasts revenue of $130bn, again excluding China. That is 12% more than in Q3, but the quarter has 14 weeks instead of 13. Roughly removing the extra week leaves about $121bn, 4% more than in Q3. Nvidia beat its Q3 forecast by 7%.

Gross margin is expected to fall to 71% to 72%. Nvidia attributed the slight margin decline in Q3 to the start of Vera Rubin production and, according to CNBC, explains the Q4 decline the same way. Operating expenses are expected to rise to $11bn, from $9.2bn in Q3.

On 16 November, according to CNBC, the US Commerce Department restricted Chinese companies' access to computing capacity in data centres outside China. The outlook does not include China.

For fiscal 2028 (February 2027 to January 2028), Kress forecast revenue growth of 75% on the call; in August Nvidia had forecast 70%. Based on the Q4 guidance, this year's revenue will be about $424bn, 96% more than last year. Growth of 75% would mean about $741bn. According to CNBC, gross margin is expected to return to 72% to 73% in FY28.

## Cash, inventory and commitments

Free cash flow rose from $21.4bn in Q2 to $43.8bn. According to the company, this was partly due to the timing of tax payments. Nvidia returned a record $28.1bn to shareholders in Q3: $22.1bn through share buybacks and $6.0bn in dividends [graf](#graf-kazalnik-delnice). The board raised the buyback authorisation by $150bn; it plans to use the remaining $227bn by the end of fiscal 2028.

At the same time, the trend we flagged as a warning in our previous analysis continues. Customers are paying more slowly and inventory is growing [graf](#graf-kazalnik-zaloge):

| At quarter end | Q1 FY27 | Q2 FY27 | Q3 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days to customer payment | 45 | 60 | 64 |
| Inventory | $25.8bn | $31.6bn | $39.7bn |
| Supplier commitments | $119bn | $279bn | $390bn |
| All commitments | | $366bn | $518bn |
| Guarantees of others' obligations | | $108.5bn | $118.0bn |

Nvidia attributes the slower payments to the timing of shipments during the Vera Rubin launch and to longer payment terms on large multi-year agreements with some investment-grade customers. It says the inventory is there to support the Vera Rubin ramp. Supplier commitments rose mainly because of multi-year memory purchases. Customers last took longer to pay from May to October 2022 (Q2 and Q3 FY23), when revenue was falling.

A **guarantee** means Nvidia will pay if a partner does not. The largest, up to $105bn, covers data centres in Ohio that OpenAI will lease. New guarantees for power and systems total $9.5bn. Nvidia has not had to pay anything so far.

Investments in other companies rose from $90.7bn to $109.7bn. On investments in private companies, Nvidia recorded a $1.8bn impairment in Q3; it does not say which companies.

## A conclusion everyone has to draw for themselves

The first report without Huang showed a record quarter. Revenue and guidance beat expectations, the operating margin is the highest in five years, and there is enough cash for record buybacks and dividends. Management also raised its growth outlook for next year. The founder's resignation did not affect this quarter's numbers, as the quarter ended on 25 October.

The warnings from our previous analysis, however, have grown stronger. Customers are paying more slowly, inventory and commitments are growing faster than revenue, more and more sales go to a group of customers that includes those Nvidia helps to finance, and gross margin will be lower in Q4. The market was nonetheless cautious and gave up the post-results gain within a day. If Nvidia does become more selective in financing customers, the very group that is now growing fastest could slow down. Those who believe demand is durable see a company growing by 100% that is cheaper than a week ago. The cautious see Nvidia increasingly carrying its customers' risk, at a time when it is looking for a new chief executive.

## Key takeaways

- Q3 FY27 revenue: $115.8bn, growth 103% (Q2: 106%); 7% above the forecast, 5% without China, 6% above analyst expectations.
- Data centre $107.6bn; other customers +195%, large cloud providers +71%.
- Gross margin 74.8%, operating income $77.5bn; $67 of each $100 of revenue remains.
- Q4 guidance: $130bn with 14 weeks (analysts $125.1bn), roughly $121bn on 13 weeks; gross margin 71% to 72%.
- For FY28 management forecasts revenue growth of 75% (70% in August).
- Three customers accounted for 36% of revenue in Q3, the largest for 14%.
- Free cash flow $43.8bn; $28.1bn returned to shareholders.
- Days to customer payment 64 (Q2: 60), inventory $39.7bn, supplier commitments $390bn.
- Huang resigned on 11 Nov; Colette Kress is interim chief executive, Harvey C. Jones chair of the board.
- Share price before the results $155.46, 35% below the peak; up to +5% after the release, about −2% during trading on 18 Nov; P/E 18.5 instead of 24.8 a week earlier.
