## Themes of the week

### 1. Tech stocks: a big drop on Thursday, yet the week ended almost flat

The week began after the Nasdaq 100's biggest weekly drop since April last year. In the week to 16 October, the S&P 500 lost almost 2% according to CNBC and the Nasdaq Composite 4.5%, ending its four-week winning streak. MarketWatch wrote at the time that the S&P 500's tech sector had its worst week in a year and a half.

On Monday, chipmakers including Micron recovered and markets ended the day mostly higher: the Nasdaq 100 gained 1.17% and, according to Yahoo Finance, had its best day in almost a month, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, an index of chipmaker shares, rose about 1.6%. CNBC wrote that this week's company earnings reports would show whether the rebound would last. Asian markets did not join in: in the middle of Tuesday's session, the MSCI Asia Pacific index of Asian shares was down 0.8% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng 1%.

On Tuesday, the Dow Jones gained 1.02%, or 530 points. CNBC wrote that traders were growing more confident about the earnings season. In the evening, according to Bloomberg, futures on US indices, which also trade outside regular market hours, rose. They were lifted by strong results from some companies that benefit from the artificial intelligence boom. On Wednesday, the Nasdaq 100 gained 1.13%.

On Thursday, the Nasdaq 100 lost 2.90% and the S&P 500 1.21%, while the Dow Jones ended the day almost unchanged. According to CNBC, it was the Nasdaq Composite's worst day since June. Yahoo Finance wrote that Meta's plan for bigger spending on data centres and equipment had revived investor concerns, as more and more tech companies borrow to fund artificial intelligence. MarketWatch wrote that chipmakers also fell because Tesla cut its planned spending and Lam Research, a maker of chipmaking equipment, issued a weaker forecast than expected. The consumer staples sector gained 3.31% on Thursday and the utilities sector 2.07%. MarketWatch wrote in the evening that the US stock market was splitting into two camps, and Yahoo Finance on Friday that the week had been marked by a shift out of AI-related stocks into defensive investments, meaning sectors less affected by swings in the economy.

On Friday, according to Yahoo Finance, the Nasdaq turned positive during trading as chip stocks rebounded. At Friday's close, the Nasdaq 100 was unchanged, the S&P 500 lost 0.52% and the Dow Jones 1.23% (more in theme 6).

Over the whole week, the Nasdaq 100 lost 0.79% and the S&P 500 0.47%, while the Dow Jones gained 0.41%. The information technology sector ended the week almost unchanged, at -0.13%. Eight of 11 sectors rose. The energy sector gained the most over the week, 1.60%, followed by health care with 1.45%. The communication services sector, which includes Meta and Netflix, lost the most over the week, 4.36%.

### 2. SpaceX turns a profit, Musk explores a merger with Tesla

Elon Musk, the head of SpaceX, announced on Monday that next year he will give one SpaceX share to every child in the US. He did not explain how.

On Tuesday after the market close, SpaceX reported results for the third quarter, July to September. The New York Times reports that the company swung from a loss to a profit, and that revenue from the Starlink satellite internet service and from artificial intelligence products more than doubled. MarketWatch says profit and revenue beat analysts' expectations. The same evening, the Financial Times reported that the boards of SpaceX and Tesla are exploring a merger. Musk said a merger would be a "good idea". According to MarketWatch, special committees at both companies are assessing the options, and a merger is not certain.

On Wednesday, according to CNBC, SpaceX shares gained 10% before the market opened. JPMorgan said the share price would triple. The New York Times writes that the combined company would be worth well over $3tn, while the Financial Times describes the merger as a $3.6tn gamble by Musk. MarketWatch warns that such a merger would very likely be a bad idea.

On Wednesday after the market close, Tesla also reported results. The Financial Times reports that profit slipped on weaker vehicle sales, while revenue stayed at last year's level. Tesla's shares fell in trading after the close. MarketWatch writes that Musk said nothing new about the merger.

### 3. The 10-year Treasury yield has its biggest daily drop since 2023

US government bonds were a refuge from the fall in tech stocks this week. The 10-year yield, the interest rate investors demand for lending to the government, stood between 5.12% and 5.15% from Monday to Wednesday. When investors buy bonds, their price rises and the yield falls.

On Thursday, MarketWatch reported during trading that investors were returning to government bonds to protect themselves against further turmoil in stock and credit markets linked to artificial intelligence. The 10-year yield fell on Thursday by 17 basis points (0.17 percentage points) to 4.96%, and the 2-year yield by 16 basis points to 4.65%. According to MarketWatch, it was the biggest daily drop in the 10-year yield since the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023, while CNBC writes that the yield was at its lowest in almost a month. Tipp of the asset manager PGIM told MarketWatch that this was not a situation driven by fear of a recession. Yahoo Finance wrote during trading on Thursday that bond traders were backing away from bets on another Fed rate hike.

Gold, which is also considered a safe investment, did not rise this time. Bloomberg wrote on Thursday evening that gold was near its lowest close in more than three months, as investors sold it in a sell-off across several markets at once. The Financial Times reported during trading on Friday that the gold price fell below $4,000 an ounce even as investors sought safety in government bonds. Gold ended the week at $4,007.90, 4.14% lower than a week earlier.

On Friday, the 10-year yield closed at 5.00% according to the US Department of the Treasury. Over the whole week it fell by 9 basis points.

*10-year US Treasury -17 bp on Thursday, 22 Oct.*

### 4. The US economy ahead of the Fed meeting: housing weakens, layoffs stay low

This week's economic data showed two sides of the US economy ahead of the meeting of the Federal Reserve, the central bank, which will decide on interest rates on 28 October. CNBC wrote on Tuesday that the Fed is weighing further rate hikes.

The housing market is weakening. On Monday, Bloomberg reported that homebuilder confidence fell to its lowest level in 14 years, with mortgage rates above 7%, labour shortages and expensive building materials. On Tuesday, data for September showed that construction of new homes was among the slowest since the pandemic (Bloomberg), while contracts to buy existing homes fell to the lowest level on record; the Financial Times says they fell 7.3% in September. CNBC writes that sales, construction and sentiment all fell more than analysts expected. The average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.32% on Thursday, according to the New York Times.

The labour market remains solid. On Thursday, new claims for unemployment benefits fell by 6,000 to 194,000, the lowest in three months according to Bloomberg; economists had expected 200,000. CNBC says claims are near their lowest level since the 1960s.

On Friday, the University of Michigan reported that consumer sentiment slipped from 48.1 points in September to 48 points in October, the lowest in five months according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg says consumers are weighed down by high prices and higher interest rates.

*Initial jobless claims 194,000, expected 200,000 (Bloomberg).*

### 5. Meta after its results: up in the evening, down the next day

Meta, the owner of Facebook and Instagram, reported third-quarter results on Wednesday after the market close. MarketWatch says the core advertising business had a strong quarter, and investors were interested in new businesses around Muse, Meta's artificial intelligence personal assistant. The shares rose in trading after the close.

The same day, CNBC reported that Meta must pay $25bn in penalties in the US state of New Mexico in the Cambridge Analytica case. Alongside its results, Meta also announced new Class C shares without voting rights. According to Yahoo Finance, this gives the company a way to raise money after free cash flow fell below zero because of heavy spending. MarketWatch and the New York Times report that the new share class was part of a deal that keeps Mark Zuckerberg in California. Because of a proposed California tax on billionaires' wealth, MarketWatch says, he had been planning to list his house in Palo Alto for sale within days. CNBC writes that the deal, less than two weeks before the midterm elections, is fuelling the debate about that tax.

On Thursday, according to CNBC, Meta's shares lost 8% during trading. The price of five-year insurance against a default on Meta's debt (CDS), meaning the price of protection against the company not repaying its debt, was reaching record levels, according to CNBC.

### 6. Brent crude 6.24% more expensive over the week amid attacks on Saudi Arabia

Oil prices this week were driven by the war with Iran and attacks on Saudi Arabia. Already on Friday, 16 October, Brent reached $110 a barrel according to the Financial Times, its highest in five months, when Saudi-led forces intercepted missiles aimed at critical energy facilities.

On Monday, according to CNBC, Saudi Arabia postponed its big investment conference to 2027 after Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels repeatedly attacked it with missiles and drones. Bloomberg wrote in the evening that traders were weighing the risk of new Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, through which oil from the Persian Gulf is shipped, against strong oil exports from the Gulf.

On Wednesday, the Financial Times, Bloomberg and MarketWatch reported that oil exports from the Persian Gulf were roughly back at their level before the war with Iran, because ships are also using other routes. The same day, the Financial Times reported that two people were killed in a drone attack on Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield and that the US ordered some of its personnel to leave Saudi Arabia. CNBC reported overnight into Thursday that the US is reportedly preparing strikes on Iran after the midterm elections.

On Thursday Brent rose 4.00%, and on Friday a further 3.14%. Over the whole week it rose from $109.86 to $116.72 a barrel. MarketWatch wrote on Thursday about a new energy squeeze caused by sharply higher tanker shipping rates. According to CNBC, President Donald Trump accused Iran that day of trying to manipulate the oil market. On Friday, according to the New York Times, Brent reached $117 a barrel during trading, the highest since April. MarketWatch wrote that oil could rise further if the US and Israel strike Iran after the elections in both countries.

The more expensive oil reached elsewhere too. On Friday the Dow Jones lost 653 points, or 1.23%; MarketWatch wrote after the close that the indices fell as oil hit its highest price in six months. The energy sector ended the day 2.60% higher. US airlines cut their full-year forecasts because of more expensive fuel (see Company results this week). UK consumer confidence fell in October by as much as after the Brexit referendum, according to Bloomberg, as high fuel prices hit consumers; the Financial Times writes that it was the biggest monthly drop since the start of the pandemic in 2020.

*Brent crude +4.00% on Thursday, 22 Oct., and +3.14% on Friday, 23 Oct.*
