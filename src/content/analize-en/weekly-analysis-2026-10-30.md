## Themes of the week

### 1. Alphabet and Amazon results trigger a tech stock sell-off

The week started with gains. On Monday, according to MarketWatch, US markets closed higher because Anthropic's filing to go public lifted shares of tech and artificial intelligence companies (theme 4). The Nasdaq 100 gained 1.09% on Monday, 1.29% on Tuesday and 0.55% on Wednesday. On Wednesday, according to Yahoo Finance, chipmaker shares around the world were also lifted by the South Korean memory chipmaker SK Hynix, which reported better results than expected.

On Wednesday after the market close, Alphabet and Microsoft reported Q3 2026 results. According to the Financial Times, Alphabet missed expectations for revenue and profit because Google search brought in less than analysts had expected, and the shares lost 9% in trading after the close on Wednesday. The Financial Times also mentions $51bn of spending on artificial intelligence. MarketWatch writes that investors were not reassured even by a doubling of cloud revenue, as they worry that artificial intelligence is eroding Google's earnings from search. Microsoft beat expectations, and its shares rose almost 3% in trading after the close on Wednesday, according to CNBC. Yahoo Finance says Microsoft spent less on investment than analysts had expected.

On Thursday, according to CNBC, Alphabet was down 10% before the market opened, and Bloomberg wrote in the middle of the day that the stock was heading for the biggest loss in the company's history. The Nasdaq 100 lost 1.38% on Thursday. After the close, Amazon reported its results. Revenue from its AWS cloud computing business grew at the fastest pace in almost five years, but according to CNBC the company missed expectations for profit and revenue, and the Financial Times writes about a weaker sales forecast for the Christmas quarter. Intel also reported the same evening (theme 2).

On Friday, according to CNBC, Amazon was down 7% before the market opened. The Financial Times wrote in the morning that big tech companies had lost almost $900bn of market value in a sell-off over soaring spending on artificial intelligence. The Nasdaq 100 lost 3.46% on Friday, the S&P 500 1.82%, the Dow Jones 1.09% and the information technology sector 4.89%. According to Yahoo Finance, it was the worst day for tech stocks since June, and according to MarketWatch the Nasdaq Composite had its worst month since March 2025.

Over the whole week, the Nasdaq 100 lost 1.98%, the S&P 500 0.57% and the Dow Jones 0.85%; it was the Nasdaq 100's third weekly loss in a row. The monthly change for October was -6.65% for the Nasdaq 100, -0.93% for the S&P 500 and +1.70% for the Dow Jones. Even so, 9 of 11 sectors rose over the week. The health care sector gained the most over the week, 3.12%, while the communication services sector, which includes Alphabet, lost 5.32% and the technology sector 2.03%. Bloomberg wrote on Friday that investors had returned to defensive stocks, meaning companies less affected by swings in the economy.

*Communication Services sector (XLC) -1.87% on Thursday, 29 Oct., -5.32% over the week; Nasdaq 100 -3.46% on Friday, 30 Oct.*

### 2. Intel loses almost a third of its value after a weak outlook, chip stocks fall

The US chipmaker Intel reported Q3 2026 results on Thursday after the market close. According to the company, revenue, margin and earnings beat its own forecast, but for the last quarter of the year it forecast revenue of $15bn to $16bn. The Financial Times writes that the company expects lower sales of PC chips than expected and lower margins, and CNBC that it blamed the weak outlook on a slowdown in the PC market. The company wrote that it expects the slowdown in that market to last at least until the middle of next year. MarketWatch says Intel's PC business is under pressure because more expensive memory chips are denting demand and corporate IT departments have less money for laptops.

The shares lost about a fifth in trading after the close: CNBC gives 21%, the Financial Times 20%. On Friday, according to CNBC, Intel was down 25% before the market opened, and MarketWatch estimated that it would lose about $128bn of market value at the opening bell. According to Bloomberg, Intel ended the day 29% lower. Yahoo Finance says the SOX index of chipmaker shares lost 10% on Friday. According to CNBC, several analysts cut their price targets for Intel's shares.

*Information Technology sector (XLK) -4.89% on Friday, 30 Oct. Intel's result is in the table Company results this week.*

### 3. The Fed and the ECB hold rates, the US economy grows at the fastest pace in a year

The US central bank, the Federal Reserve, left interest rates unchanged on Wednesday, after raising them in September for the first time since 2023, according to Bloomberg. According to a CNBC survey, 93% of respondents expected a pause. The decision was taken by 10 votes to 2; MarketWatch reports that the two members who voted against wanted a hike. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said inflation is still too high and the Fed will deliver price stability, and that there was no hike because a period with a lot of new data is coming before the December meeting. He also announced fewer rate-setting meetings: seven in 2027 and six in 2028, compared with eight a year so far (New York Times). Bloomberg says the Fed has stayed on track for another hike this year. On Friday, three Fed officials backed further hikes, and Alberto Musalem, president of the St. Louis Fed, said another hike is needed soon.

Bloomberg wrote on Wednesday that yields on short-term US Treasuries fell after the decision; the 2-year yield fell by 3 basis points that day and by 13 over the whole week.

On Thursday, the US Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) reported that gross domestic product growth in Q3 2026 was 3.4% (annualised), compared with 2.2% in Q2 2026. According to the New York Times, the economy grew at its fastest pace in a year, and CNBC writes that growth was higher than expected. Year-on-year price growth by the PCE measure, which the Fed uses for its target, was 3.6% in September, according to Yahoo Finance. On Tuesday, Bloomberg reported that US consumer confidence rose in October for the first time in four months.

The European Central Bank also held rates on Thursday (Financial Times). Its president Christine Lagarde announced the same day that she will step down in February, before the end of her term. Bloomberg writes that the race to succeed her has begun. On Friday, the Bank of Japan held rates and, according to the Financial Times, signalled further increases.

*2-year US Treasury -3 bp on Wednesday, 28 Oct., -13 bp over the week.*

### 4. Anthropic files to go public

The artificial intelligence company Anthropic published a prospectus on Monday for its initial public offering, in which it will offer its shares to investors on the stock market for the first time. The Financial Times writes that the document runs to 335 pages. CNBC says the company has $6.2bn of debt and lists bans on building data centres among its key risks. MarketWatch wrote after the close that markets ended the day higher because the filing lifted shares of tech and artificial intelligence companies, and Bloomberg reported in the evening that the gains spread to Asian markets.

On Tuesday, MarketWatch wrote that shares of CoreWeave and other artificial intelligence companies rose because Anthropic had restored the market's faith that spending on artificial intelligence will continue. According to unofficial Bloomberg reports, Anthropic picked JPMorgan to lead the offering to retail investors in the US, and the New York Times reports on a contest between Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley for the lead role. CNBC says traders on prediction markets consider the offering almost certain this year.

*Nasdaq 100 +1.09% on Monday, 26 Oct.*

### 5. Oil between diplomacy and threats against Iran, oil companies with big profits

Oil prices swung this week between signs of diplomacy and signs of new military strikes on Iran. Brent crude ended the week at $117.64 a barrel, 0.79% higher than a week earlier; in October it rose 20%.

At the end of the previous week, according to Bloomberg, the US ordered some diplomatic staff to leave embassies in the Middle East because of Iranian plans to attack Americans and threats from Yemen's Houthi rebels. On Sunday, Saudi Arabia said it had intercepted Houthi missiles and drones (CNBC).

On Monday, Brent lost 0.78%. Bank of America, according to Francisco Blanch, expects oil at around $120 a barrel if Iran again attacks energy infrastructure in the region. Bloomberg wrote on Monday evening that prices were falling for a second day as stepped-up diplomatic efforts raised hopes that a wider conflict between the US and Iran could be avoided. On Tuesday, Brent lost 1.67%.

On Tuesday evening, Bloomberg reported that oil rose after the Wall Street Journal reported that the US had told several Persian Gulf states to prepare for a new military campaign against Iran after the midterm elections. On Wednesday, Brent gained 1.26%. On Wednesday evening, according to Bloomberg, oil fell again on signs of new diplomacy, and on Thursday Brent lost 0.38%. On Thursday evening, according to Bloomberg, it rose on signs that the US could resume military strikes on Iran. On Friday, according to CNBC, Iran claimed that four tankers had hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and the Financial Times wrote about Thursday's attack on a tanker with limpet mines, which attackers attach to a ship, the first such attack since 2019. Brent gained 2.40% on Friday.

High fuel prices brought oil companies big profits. The Financial Times wrote on Thursday that European oil companies are increasing their share buybacks, and Shell raised its buyback to $5bn, according to Bloomberg. Exxon Mobil and Chevron reported very high profits on Friday thanks to strong refining earnings and warned of a diesel shortage, according to the Financial Times; according to CNBC, both shares reached all-time highs. The energy sector gained 1.91% on Friday.

*Brent crude +2.40% on Friday, 30 Oct., +0.79% over the week.*

### 6. Gold below $4,000, bitcoin down, crypto exchange Binance robbed

Gold held at around $4,000 an ounce on Monday and Tuesday, according to Bloomberg, and the asset manager Amundi said on Tuesday that the worst was over. After Wednesday's Fed decision, gold edged lower, according to Bloomberg, because the Fed gave no clear signal about its next move. On Thursday evening, Bloomberg wrote that gold was heading for its worst October since 2008 as the sell-off in AI-related stocks spread. On Friday, gold lost 3.29% and ended the week at $3,898.90 an ounce, 2.72% lower than a week earlier. MarketWatch wrote after the close that this was the lowest price in a year, despite renewed tensions around Iran. The monthly change for gold in October was -6.87%.

Bitcoin lost 4.27% on Thursday and ended the week at $77,180, 4.81% lower than a week earlier. On Thursday evening, CNBC reported that Circle and Tether, issuers of stablecoins, meaning cryptocurrencies pegged to the dollar, had frozen funds linked to a potential security problem at the crypto exchange Binance. On Friday, the Financial Times reported that Binance had halted withdrawals after a record $6.3bn theft, and the New York Times that shares of crypto companies were falling during trading. CNBC wrote on Friday that bitcoin had held up relatively well after the hack.

*Gold -3.29% on Friday, 30 Oct.; bitcoin -4.27% on Thursday, 29 Oct.*
