## Themes of the week

### 1. Jensen Huang steps down as Nvidia's chief executive

Before the week began, Nvidia's board launched an independent investigation after chief executive and co-founder Jensen Huang admitted a relationship with a woman whom US intelligence considers a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares fell 5% before the open on Monday, according to CNBC, while the rest of the market rose (theme 3). Nvidia entrusted the investigation to the law firm Wachtell Lipton, and the shares lost another 5.5% on Tuesday, according to Yahoo Finance.

On Wednesday evening, according to CNBC and the New York Times, the Wall Street Journal reported that the board was considering removing Huang, and the shares fell more than 8% in after-hours trading. On Thursday morning Huang resigned, and chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim chief executive (Financial Times). The shares fell 5% on Thursday, according to CNBC, and according to MarketWatch were already 30% below their peak. On Friday they kept falling: according to MarketWatch, Nvidia lost $657 billion of market value over the week, and according to Bloomberg it had its worst week since April 2025. The Financial Times reported that Arm chief executive Rene Haas had been approached about the top job. William Blair raised its rating on AMD, which could gain from the leadership vacuum at Nvidia, according to CNBC; according to Bloomberg, AMD and Broadcom shares were already rising on Wednesday evening on bets that they would win some of Nvidia's business.

Nvidia makes up about 6% of the S&P 500, according to Yahoo Finance, so the affair weighed on the whole market. According to the New York Times, Senator Tim Scott asked on Thursday evening for Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify before the Senate Banking Committee, and according to CNBC Lutnick agreed. According to the Financial Times, lawmakers want Lutnick to explain why he allowed chip sales to a company linked to the alleged spy. Nvidia will report results on Tuesday, 17 November. More on the company in our [analysis of Nvidia after the fall](/en/analysis/nvidia-analysis-2026-11-12/).

### 2. Inflation above expectations, markets bet on a December rate hike

On Tuesday morning Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he was leaning towards a rate hike in December and would also support a half-point increase if inflation was high (CNBC). The Financial Times wrote that this would be the biggest increase since 2022.

A few hours later the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported that consumer prices rose 0.4% in October in a single month, and prices excluding food and energy (core inflation) 0.3%. Analysts had expected 0.3% and 0.2%, according to CNBC. This time the BLS does not publish year-on-year rates, because it did not collect data in October 2025 during the federal government shutdown. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the report "borderline disturbing", according to Bloomberg, and San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said on Thursday that further hikes would be needed.

Treasury yields rose sharply. The two-year yield, which tracks expectations for Fed interest rates most closely, ended the week at 4.88%, 37 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) higher than a week earlier; on Tuesday alone it rose 17 points. According to Bloomberg, yields on Tuesday jumped the most since the tariff turmoil of April 2025. The ten-year yield ended the week at 5.15%, 29 points higher, and touched 5.17% during trading on Friday, according to CNBC. There is no yield figure for Wednesday, the US Veterans Day holiday. MarketWatch noted that cheaper oil is no longer pulling yields down, a sign that inflation concerns reach beyond energy.

On Friday the BLS reported that producer prices rose 0.3% in October in a single month and 6.5% over the year, the most since November 2022. Bloomberg wrote on Friday that the Fed is preparing to raise rates in December and the debate is moving to January; according to Bloomberg, the dollar had its best week in more than a month.

Gold fell 4.63% over the week to $3,886.30 an ounce, weighed down by bets on a rate hike, according to Bloomberg. Bitcoin lost 9.90% over the week to $68,237, 4.39% on Friday alone. MarketWatch wrote that it could fall further if investors sell bitcoin to buy Anthropic shares.

### 3. Stocks rebound, CoreWeave cheers AI investors

After last week's fall in tech stocks, Monday brought a rebound. The S&P 500 gained 2.35% on Monday, its biggest one-day rise since April according to MarketWatch, the Nasdaq 100 3.40% and the Dow Jones 2.15% or 1,104 points. According to Bloomberg, the S&P 500 erased its losses since the midterm elections. On Tuesday the indices fell after the inflation report, the S&P 500 by 0.38%.

On Wednesday the Nasdaq 100 gained 2.28% and the information technology sector 3.23%. CoreWeave, which rents out computing power for artificial intelligence, reported revenue above expectations after the close on Tuesday and raised its annual guidance (CNBC), and its shares jumped 24% on Wednesday morning, according to Yahoo Finance. On Thursday the Nasdaq opened sharply lower because of Nvidia, according to MarketWatch, but ended the day only 0.30% lower, and the S&P 500 0.35% lower.

Japan's SoftBank, which has large investments in artificial intelligence, reported a $7 billion loss on Tuesday after writing down the value of a US data centre and a fall in the value of its Intel shares (CNBC, Financial Times); its shares had risen 13% in Tokyo that day, before the results, according to CNBC, as Asian tech stocks followed the Wall Street rebound.

On Friday tech stocks fell again as, according to MarketWatch, the market reassesses AI investment without Huang: the S&P 500 lost 0.40%, the Nasdaq 100 1.22% and the information technology sector 2.01%, while the Dow Jones gained 0.10%. Over the week the S&P 500 rose 2.51%, the Nasdaq 100 3.53% and the Dow Jones 2.07%. The strongest sectors were energy (5.42%), industrials (3.76%) and communication services (3.72%).

### 5. Oil swings with Trump's statements on Iran

Brent, the European benchmark crude, ended the day 5.85% lower. On Monday Exxon said it had reached a preliminary agreement to return to Venezuela (New York Times).

On Friday Brent was almost unchanged, and over the week it fell 2.52% to $95.84. The energy sector of the S&P 500 gained 5.42% over the week, the most of all sectors.
