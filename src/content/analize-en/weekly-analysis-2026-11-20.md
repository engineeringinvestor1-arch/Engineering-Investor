A review of market events from 16 to 20 November 2026: key numbers, sectors, the main themes of the week, company results and the calendar for the next two weeks.

US stocks had their worst week since April 2025. The S&P 500 lost 8.61% and closed in a correction on Friday, more than 10% below its peak, while the Nasdaq 100 lost 10.43%. We explained the reasons for the declines up to Thursday in the article [Why markets are falling](/en/analysis/markets-2026-11-20/): an expected interest rate hike, doubts about artificial intelligence and selling of assets for cash. This review adds Friday, when the decline deepened, and the themes we did not cover there: the statements of Fed officials, the investigations into Nvidia, Anthropic's first two days on the stock market, the wave of tech outages and the APEC summit.

## Themes of the week

### 1. The S&P 500 and Dow Jones in a correction, the Nasdaq erases its gains for the year

On Friday the sell-off deepened. The S&P 500 lost 3.70% and fell for a seventh day in a row, the Nasdaq 100 lost 5.61% and the Dow Jones 1.93%. The S&P 500 closed 11.6% below its 6 October peak and the Dow Jones 11.4% below its 5 August peak. Markets call a fall of more than 10% from a peak a **correction**, and according to MarketWatch both indices ended the week in one. The broader Nasdaq Composite lost 5.28% on Friday and erased all of its gains for the year (Yahoo Finance). The Nasdaq 100 is 19.8% below its 6 October peak, just short of the 20% threshold at which markets speak of a **bear market**, a more lasting decline; the Financial Times wrote that US tech stocks had already fallen into one.

Over the week the S&P 500 lost 8.61%, the most since April 2025 (Yahoo Finance). The Nasdaq 100 lost 10.43% and the Dow Jones 8.02%; for both it was the worst week since March 2020 (MarketWatch, Yahoo Finance). According to MarketWatch, all 11 S&P 500 sectors ended the week more than 1% lower, for the first time since April 2025. Information technology lost the most (12.49%) and consumer staples the least (2.05%); staples were the only sector to rise on Friday. The PHLX Semiconductor index, which tracks chipmakers, lost 17.13% over the week, 10.73% on Friday alone.

The **VIX index**, which measures expected stock market swings over the next month and is known as the fear index, closed at 35.89 on Friday, the highest since April 2025 (Bloomberg). A week earlier it was at 19.30. According to Bloomberg, Goldman Sachs wrote that its panic gauge was close to its maximum level, which its traders read as a sign that another wave of selling will follow next week.

The declines spread around the world. Taiwan's stock index lost 17.50% over the week; on Friday morning Bloomberg was already writing that it was the worst week since 1991. Japan's Nikkei lost 7.70%, South Korea's Kospi 7.95% and Europe's Euro Stoxx 50 6.97%. Shares of Japan's SoftBank, a large investor in OpenAI, fell more than 16% on Friday, according to CNBC.

The selling of assets that can be turned into cash quickly, which we wrote about in the [article on the market declines](/en/analysis/markets-2026-11-20/), also continued. Gold fell 9.54% over the week to $3,515.40 an ounce, the most since March according to Bloomberg. Bitcoin lost 13.81% and fell below $60,000 on Friday (Yahoo Finance). According to Bloomberg, copper fell to its lowest price since March.

### 2. Fed officials signal more hikes, yet bonds rallied

The US central bank, the Fed, kept its key interest rate in a range of 3.75% to 4% in October, and markets expect a hike in December. This week Fed officials spoke almost every day. On Monday, Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said she prefers small and gradual hikes but that larger ones may be needed (Bloomberg). On Tuesday, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said, according to CNBC, that four more hikes may be needed in the coming months; Bloomberg summarised his words as "several" hikes by spring. New York Fed President John Williams said the same day that he saw no need for "dramatic action" (Bloomberg).

On Wednesday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed should consider a half-point hike in December and that it may need to "shock" people (CNBC). The same day the Fed published the minutes of its October meeting. Almost all officials agreed that interest rates would likely have to rise "in the months ahead" to bring inflation back to the 2% target in a timely manner (Bloomberg). They were also worried that building infrastructure for artificial intelligence would raise inflation (CNBC, Financial Times). On Thursday, Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said he would consider all options in December and did not rule out a larger hike (Bloomberg). On Friday, Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said she would be open to hikes at consecutive meetings, but at a deliberate pace (Bloomberg). On Monday, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said Fed staff were preparing scenarios for the impact of artificial intelligence on the economy (Bloomberg). CNBC wrote on Friday that Warsh wants officials to speak less in public, but they are speaking more and more.

Despite the hawkish statements, US Treasury yields fell over the week. The **yield** is a bond's annual return at its current price: when investors buy bonds, the price rises and the yield falls. The 10-year yield fell from 5.15% to 4.92%, by 23 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point), 12 of them on Friday. The 2-year yield, which tracks expectations for Fed rates most closely, fell from 4.88% to 4.63%. According to Bloomberg, investors fled from stocks into bonds, which are considered a safe investment. On Friday, according to Bloomberg, the Japanese yen and the Swiss franc, which investors buy in uncertain times, also strengthened. The dollar nevertheless ended the week 0.39% higher.

On Friday the University of Michigan reported that US consumer sentiment improved in November: the index rose from 48.0 to 51.5. The inflation consumers expect over the next year fell from 4.7% to 3.7%, and long-run expected inflation from 3.5% to 3.3%. Consumers with the largest stockholdings reported weaker personal finances, which the authors of the survey link to the AI-related turmoil on the stock market.

### 5. A wave of tech outages: Salesforce and banks down, cybersecurity up

### 6. Trump, Xi and Putin in Shenzhen: plenty of symbolism, few agreements

US President Donald Trump began a three-day trip to Asia on Tuesday and said he was open to a one-on-one meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin (Bloomberg). At the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, Chinese President Xi Jinping called for lower barriers to trade and investment and promised broader access to the Chinese market (CNBC). He warned against "rampant" protectionism, just days after the US further restricted China's access to the most advanced AI chips (Bloomberg). Putin stressed the resilience of the Russian economy despite US sanctions and said the European Union is losing industry (CNBC). As the summit began, China's central bank set the strongest yuan reference rate since 2022 (Bloomberg).

On Thursday Trump met Putin and Xi separately. After meeting Putin he praised their "great relationship", with the war in Ukraine taking centre stage (CNBC). At the trilateral meeting the leaders pledged to boost global fuel supplies, but otherwise, according to Bloomberg, the meeting produced little of substance. Bloomberg Economics analyst Adam Farrar had described it as a photo opportunity before it began.
